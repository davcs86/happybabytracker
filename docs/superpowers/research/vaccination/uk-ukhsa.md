Not listed anywhere for ages 0 to 18 in routine rows: MenC (removed), BCG, hepatitis B monovalent (selective only), COVID-19, RSV (pregnancy and 75+ only), shingles (65+), pneumococcal PPV23/PCV20 (65+ or risk groups).

### Selective programmes in the same edition (0 to 18 relevant)
- "Babies born to women with hepatitis B infection": "At birth and four weeks" hepatitis B (Engerix B/HBvaxPRO); footnote: "In addition hexavalent vaccine (Infanrix hexa or Vaxelis) is given at 8, 12 and 16 weeks and also at 18 months." and "Take blood for HBsAg anytime between 12 and 18 months to test for infection." (NHS page: monovalent HepB "also at 12 months for babies born on or before 30 June 2024".)
- BCG: "Within 28 days of birth" for infants in areas with TB incidence >= 40/100,000, or with a parent/grandparent born in a high-incidence country; footnote "Check SCID screening outcome before giving BCG."
- "Children in a clinical risk group", influenza: "Aged 6 months to under 2 years: if first ever flu vaccine give second dose at 4 weeks" (inactivated); "Aged 2 to 8 years: if first ever flu vaccine give second dose at 4 weeks" (LAIV, else inactivated); "Aged 9 to 17 years: only one dose required each flu season".
- Clinical-risk-group rows (asplenia MenACWY/MenB/PCV20/flu, cochlear implants PCV20, etc.) refer to the Green Book for doses; out of scope for the app.

### Recurring / "may not be needed" lines
- Annual flu "each year from September": recurring, eligibility set by the annual flu letter. NHS: "Children's flu vaccine (every year until children finish Year 11 of secondary school)", listed under "2 to 15 years".
- No "may not be needed" wording exists in the UKHSA schedule or the NHS page. The closest items are: 18 months "No appointment" for born <= 30 June 2024; Td/IPV "(check MMR status)"; footnote about SCID screening before rotavirus and BCG.
- Green Book ch 11: "Immunisations should not be given before the scheduled age unless there is a clear clinical indication"; "The first set of primary immunisations can be given from six weeks of age if required in certain circumstances, such as travel to an endemic country"; "MMR vaccine can be given from six months of age... MMRV vaccine can be given from nine months of age. Any dose of MMR or MMRV given below the age of one year should be discounted"; MMRV doses "a minimum of 4 weeks apart" (MMRV HCP page).

### Dose numbering evidence (the schedule itself does not number doses; numbers are derived)
- NHS page numbers: 6-in-1 1st/2nd/3rd at 8/12/16 weeks; 4th "for children born on or after 1 July 2024" at 18 months; rotavirus 1st/2nd; MenB 1st at 8 weeks, 2nd at 12 weeks, "3rd dose" at 1 year; pneumococcal "1st dose" at 16 weeks, "2nd dose" at 1 year; MMRV "1st dose for children born on or after 1 January 2025" at 1 year; at 18 months "MMRV vaccine (1st or 2nd dose for children born on or after 1 July 2024)"; at 3y4m "4-in-1 pre-school booster vaccine" and "MMRV vaccine (1st or 2nd dose for children born between 1 September 2022 and 31 December 2024)".
- Green Book ch 11 Table 2 (catch-up checklist) gives expected counts: by 12 months three doses D/T/P/polio/Hib/HepB-containing, one PCV, two MenB; by 24 months "either 2 doses of MenB under the age of one and one dose after the age of one year; or 2 doses of MenB after the age of one year", one Hib dose "given after the age of one year", one PCV dose after age one, at least one MMR/MMRV dose after one year; at school entry "at least four doses of diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis and polio containing vaccine", "at least three doses of hepatitis B-containing vaccine", "at least two doses of MMR-containing vaccine (as MMR or MMRV) after the age of one year (with at least one dose having been given over 18 months of age)"; before leaving school "at least five doses of diphtheria, tetanus, polio containing vaccine", "a single dose of MenACWY vaccine after the age of 10 years", "two doses of MMR-containing vaccine", "a single dose of HPV vaccine".

## 2. Proposed machine-readable form

Design choices (owner to confirm):
- Rows are per antigen per series number. A multi-antigen product (6-in-1, MMRV) becomes one row per antigen so the app can match by count per antigen. `product` is optional context only.
- Series numbers are DERIVED by counting (the source gives them only for some products). Cohorts matter: where the same antigen has a different count depending on date of birth, rows carry `cohort` (birth-date condition from the source). The app must pick the cohort rows by the child's date of birth, otherwise doses are mis-numbered. Alternative simplification: ship only the "current cohort" (born on or after 1 Jan 2025) plus the legacy cohorts as separate rows, as below.
- Months: weeks converted at 30.4375 days per month and rounded to 2 dp (8 w = 1.84; 12 w = 2.76; 16 w = 3.68; 6 w = 1.38). `recommended_to_months: null` means the SOURCE STATES NO UPPER BOUND; do not invent one. Any soft "overdue" threshold is the owner's choice, not an authority statement.
- `source_text` is the original wording.

### Ages 6 to 18 summary
- 12 to 13 years: HPV, one dose (Gardasil 9).
- 14 years (school Year 9): Td/IPV (3-in-1 teenage booster; diphtheria low dose, tetanus, polio, no pertussis) and MenACWY (single dose after age 10; free until 25th birthday).
- Annual flu to end of Year 11 if eligible that season.
- Catch-up: MenB for specific young cohorts until March 2027 (born 1 Sep 2007 to 31 Aug 2008, and some starting university/FE) per NHS page; MMR "2 doses ... before starting university"; MenACWY, HPV up to 25th birthday. These are campaigns, not routine rows.

- The gov.uk pages fetched specify NO bespoke attribution statement, so the default applies: **"Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0."** (link to the licence URL above).
- OGL exemptions (quoted/paraphrased from the licence text): personal data; information not published with the provider's consent; "departmental or public sector organisation logos, crests and the Royal Arms except where they form an integral part of a document or dataset"; third-party rights the provider cannot license; other IP rights (patents, trade marks, design rights); identity documents. Also: no use suggesting official status or endorsement; the information is licensed "as is" with no warranty ("not liable for any errors or omissions"). Vaccine trade names (Infanrix hexa, Vaxelis, Bexsero, ProQuad, etc.) are third-party trade marks; the proposed data file does not need them, so omit them.
- My reading: a transcribed table of ages and antigens is an adaptation of OGL Information, which OGL allows. Embedding it in a public MIT repository and a public container image is permitted provided the attribution line travels with the data (data file header, README/NOTICE, and ideally the in-app screen that shows the schedule). MIT can cover the app code; the data file should state that it is OGL-licensed content (OGL is compatible with CC-BY 4.0 per the licence text). Not legal advice.

## 4. Traps for a transcriber

1. Cohort-dependent dose counts. 18-month appointment exists only for "born on or after 1 July 2024"; the 3y4m MMRV only for "born on or before 31 December 2024". The complete schedule's one-year row does not show the cohort split: cohorts born 1 July 2024 to 31 Dec 2024 and earlier had MMR (not MMRV) at 12 months (MMRV HCP Table 1). Anyone born before 1 Jan 2025 whose app data says "MMRV at 1 year" is wrong.
2. Born 1 July to 31 Dec 2024 children get THREE MMR-containing doses (MMR at 12 m, MMRV 18 m, MMRV 3y4m). Count-based matching must not flag the third dose as an error or require it for "two doses".
3. Hib/MenC (Menitorix) at 12 months existed until the cohort born on or before 30 June 2024 and is no longer available; it is not in the current table but older children's records contain it. MenC is gone from the infant schedule; it is NOT replaced for those children (they rely on MenACWY at 14). Map old Hib/MenC records to two antigens (Hib dose 4, MenC dose 1) or record them as legacy.
4. Infant timing change on 1 July 2025: MenB 2nd dose moved from 16 to 12 weeks; PCV 1st moved from 12 to 16 weeks. Children who had their 12-week PCV before 1 July 2025 stayed on the old schedule. A PCV at 12 weeks given in error "still count[s] as a valid dose".
5. Age expressed as school year: "Fourteen years old (school Year 9)", flu "until children finish Year 11". NHS says "12 to 13 years" for HPV; I did not find "Year 8" in a fetched page.
6. "On or after the child's first birthday" at one year, and "Three years four months old or soon after": the 3y4m age is "from", not a window; there is no stated upper bound.
7. Doses before the first birthday of MMR/MMRV are discounted; first primary set may be given from 6 weeks only in special cases; maximum ages (rotavirus) exist but were not retrieved.
8. Product differences: 6-in-1 (D, T, aP, polio, Hib, HepB) vs 4-in-1 dTaP/IPV (no Hib, no HepB) vs 3-in-1 Td/IPV (no pertussis). A count of "pertussis doses" is 3 (6-in-1) +1 (18 m, NEW cohort) +1 (3y4m); the teenage dose adds none.
9. Doc drift between editions: Green Book ch 11 (30 Mar 2026) table lacks MMRV at 3y4m; the Sept 2026 complete schedule includes it. The complete schedule PDF says "Pneumococcal (13 serotypes) PCV Prevenar 13" even though the update log says PCV20 from 1 January 2026 (PCV20 appears only in risk-group rows). Treat PCV13 as the routine infant vaccine per the table, but flag as a possible inconsistency.
10. NHS page footer review date is stale (see section 0). The HTML slug "...from-1-july-2026" is stale.
11. Selective items overlap routine counts: hepatitis B-pathway infants get extra monovalent doses (birth, 4 weeks; 12 months only if born on or before 30 June 2024); BCG at "within 28 days" for risk groups; clinical-risk flu has its own timing (6 months+, second dose at 4 weeks if first-ever).
12. MMRV selective catch-up (1 Nov 2026 to 31 Mar 2028) is defined by date of birth (1 Jan 2020 to 31 Aug 2022), including children older than 6, and only for those without chickenpox infection or two varicella doses; MMR/MMRV minimum interval is 4 weeks.
13. Catch-up wording: "where any course of immunisation is interrupted, there is normally no need to start the course again – it should simply be resumed and completed as soon as possible" (Green Book ch 11). This fits the app's "soft overdue flag, no validity judgement" model.
14. Devolved nations: this is the England page ("Applies to England" on the supporting pages); Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own letters/dates (search results indicated differences, not fetched).
